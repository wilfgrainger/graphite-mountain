# Sharing Graphite Mountain publicly

Graphite Mountain should be presented as a personal open-source project, not as an employer method, client deliverable, or official adaptation of another organisation's framework.

## Positioning

Lead with:

- the problem: agent work can become fragmented, shallow, or overconfident;
- the contribution: six distinct expert lenses coordinated through one four-stage lifecycle;
- the differentiator: evidence, decision gates, ownership, independent review, deployment truth, and human judgment;
- the invitation: try it, challenge it, and contribute.

Avoid claims that you invented the general software lifecycle or multi-agent collaboration. Claim the specific Graphite Mountain implementation, role system, workflow, prompts, and governance model.

## Fan reference and name-use check

Keep the fan reference secondary to the original delivery method. The safest public
boundary is text-only, original writing, professional role names first, and character
aliases only as clearly labelled compatibility or cultural references.

Before sharing, verify:

- no show logo, still, clip, music, script, copied dialogue, catchphrase, actor likeness, voice imitation, or episode-specific material is present;
- the product name, repository name, domain, social handles, metadata, and logo do not imply sponsorship or official adaptation;
- the disclaimer is visible near the relevant reference and the project is not marketed as endorsed by the show, network, creators, cast, or rights holders;
- “parody” is not being used as a legal conclusion or marketing shield;
- commercial, sponsored, paid, or high-visibility distribution has received jurisdiction-specific IP and publicity-rights review;
- employment, contributor, client, and open-source licence obligations have been checked separately.

For a preliminary U.S. issue-spot only, the Copyright Office says names, titles, and
short phrases are generally not protected by copyright, while the USPTO warns that
similar marks used with related goods or services can create likelihood-of-confusion
problems. The Supreme Court has also held that parody and commentary are not
categorically insulated when a mark is used as a source identifier. Those sources do
not clear this project, and rules differ outside the U.S. See the [Copyright Office
Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf), [USPTO likelihood-of-confusion
guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion), and [Jack
Daniel’s Properties, Inc. v. VIP Products LLC](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/22pdf/22-148_3e04.pdf).

## Employment-safe checklist

Before posting:

- confirm the work was created under circumstances consistent with your employment agreement and internal intellectual-property and conflict policies;
- remove employer-confidential information, customer material, internal terminology, screenshots, prompts, templates, metrics, and non-public methods;
- do not imply employer sponsorship, review, endorsement, or product status;
- do not use employer logos or brand styling;
- keep the repository vendor-neutral;
- add a clear personal-project disclaimer;
- ask your management or legal/compliance channel when uncertain.

Suggested profile-safe disclaimer:

> Personal open-source project. Views and work are my own; this is not an employer project or endorsement.

## Posting guidance

- Use one clean repository screenshot or a simple original diagram, not television imagery.
- Avoid tagging an employer or customers.
- Describe Graphite Mountain in its own terms; do not attribute its method to an employer or vendor.
- Invite critique rather than claiming the framework is complete or industry-defining.
- Mention `v0.0.1` so the audience understands it is an initial public release.
- Keep the fan reference secondary to the engineering value.
- Do not describe a specific model or reasoning setting unless the exact run record supports it; use a capability profile otherwise.
- Treat committed `.lock.yml` files as executable automation. Disable schedules before sharing a fork unless the owner has reviewed secrets, labels, budget, and workflow permissions.
